About Stoneham

Why Stoneham's Rocky Landscape Demands Expert Concrete

Stoneham is a thriving suburban community of over 23,200 residents in Middlesex County, uniquely defined by the 2,200-acre Middlesex Fells Reservation, 298-acre Spot Pond, and Stone Zoo. With rocky glacial hills, abrupt soil transitions from bedrock to peat, and the charming Central Square Historic District, Stoneham demands concrete work that navigates challenging terrain while honoring the town's character.

Rocky Hills, Glacial Till & Peat Deposits

Stoneham's geology is notable for abrupt transitions from rock to deep peat deposits — an unstable material that retains significant groundwater. Rocky hills with diabase dikes are common throughout town, and glacial till from mile-thick ice sheets thousands of years ago creates variable soil conditions from one property to the next. We assess every site individually, often encountering bedrock, boulders, or saturated peat within feet of each other.

51 Inches of Snow & 17°F Winter Lows

Stoneham averages 51 inches of snow per year with winter lows reaching 17°F. Combined with 50 inches of annual rainfall, road salt, and plow traffic on Main Street and Route 28, outdoor surfaces endure extreme seasonal stress. Our air-entrained concrete with reinforced sub-bases is engineered to handle these demanding freeze-thaw cycles year after year.

Middlesex Fells, Spot Pond & Stone Zoo

The Middlesex Fells Reservation — one of America's oldest and largest urban state parks — covers over 2,200 acres across Stoneham and neighboring towns. Spot Pond, a 298-acre Great Pond named by Governor John Winthrop in 1632, sits almost entirely within Stoneham. Stone Zoo adds family appeal. Properties near these resources may fall within wetland buffers requiring Conservation Commission filing.

Central Square Historic District & 18th-Century Homes

Stoneham's Central Square Historic District features 19th and early 20th-century commercial architecture — the Dow Block, Oddfellows Building, and Chase Block. Historic homes include the Enoch Fuller Octagon House (c. 1850), John Botume House (1858, now the Fells Visitor Center), and multiple 18th-century residences. We match patterns and colors to complement every architectural era.
