About Tewksbury

Why Tewksbury's Terrain Demands Expert Concrete Work

Tewksbury is a thriving town of over 31,000 residents in Middlesex County, strategically located at the junction of I-93 and I-495. With the Merrimack River as its northern boundary, the Shawsheen River winding through town, an 800-acre state hospital campus, and rich Colonial history dating to 1637, Tewksbury offers diverse landscapes that require expert engineering.

Glacial Till, Boulders & Stony Soils

Tewksbury's soils were shaped by ice sheets 14,000 to 22,000 years ago. The glacial till is a mix of unsorted rubble — boulders, rocks, sandy deposits, and compact clay — that varies dramatically across properties. Eastern Massachusetts end-moraine deposits leave more boulders and rocks near the surface. A loam layer overlies these glacial features, but the stony till below dictates how we engineer every sub-base.

55 Inches of Snow & 16°F Winter Lows

Tewksbury averages 55 inches of snow per year with January lows reaching 16°F. Add 50 inches of annual rainfall, heavy road salt usage on Route 38 and the highway corridors, and constant plow traffic, and outdoor surfaces face extreme seasonal stress. Our air-entrained concrete with salt-resistant sealers is engineered to handle these demanding conditions.

Shawsheen River, Merrimack River & Wetlands

The 26.7-mile Shawsheen River winds through Tewksbury, and the Merrimack River — New England's fourth-largest watershed — forms the northern boundary. The Conservation Commission enforces the MA Wetlands Protection Act and Tewksbury's local Wetlands Protection Bylaw with a 100-foot buffer zone from wetland resource areas and vernal pools. We handle all environmental permitting through Tewksbury's OpenGov system.

Town Common, Hospital Campus & Colonial Heritage

Tewksbury's Town Common is bordered by the Colonial Revival Town Hall (1920) and the Congregational Church. The 800-acre Tewksbury Hospital campus (est. 1852) features Queen Anne, Romanesque Revival, and Classical Revival architecture. The Anne Sullivan–Helen Keller Monument on Main Street and historic homes like the Rev. Spaulding House (1737) add to the town's rich character.

North Tewksbury & Wamesit

North Tewksbury borders the Merrimack River, home to the Wamesit Indian historical site, the Merrimack River Interceptor Trail, and the Public Health Museum on the 800-acre Tewksbury Hospital campus. Properties near the river often have high water tables and flood zone considerations. The Shawsheen River also enters town here. Town Center & Route 38 Corridor

Tewksbury's Town Center is anchored by the Town Common, the Colonial Revival Town Hall (1920), and the Congregational Church. Main Street (Route 38) runs through the heart of town, connecting to both I-93 and I-495. Homes near the center are a mix of historic colonials and mid-century capes, with glacial till close to the surface requiring careful sub-base engineering.

Silver Lake, Indian Ridge & Evergreen Estates

These popular residential neighborhoods in south and east Tewksbury feature newer construction, spacious lots, and family-friendly living. Silver Lake homes tend toward higher property values. Indian Ridge and Evergreen Estates offer condominium and single-family options.
